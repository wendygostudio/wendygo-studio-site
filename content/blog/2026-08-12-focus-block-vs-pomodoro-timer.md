# Focus Block vs Pomodoro Timer: Which Unit Fits Deep Work?

A timer measures attention. A focus block measures the work you want to finish. They are related, but they are not the same unit. Choosing the right one can make a deep-work routine easier to start and easier to sustain.

## What a Pomodoro timer measures

A Pomodoro timer answers: **How long will I protect my attention?** A 25-minute session is a useful boundary when the task is vague, your energy is low or you are trying to restart a habit. The countdown creates a small commitment and a clear stopping point.

The timer is especially useful for:

- starting a task that feels too large;
- protecting a short research or writing session;
- building a repeatable daily rhythm;
- taking deliberate breaks before attention drops.

## What a focus block measures

A focus block answers: **What observable result will exist when I stop?** The block might be “draft the introduction”, “review ten log entries” or “prepare the first image crop”. Its duration can be 15 or 45 minutes, but the outcome remains the anchor.

Blocks work well for deep tasks because they prevent the timer from becoming the goal. Completing four hours of countdowns is not useful if the work never moves forward. A clear outcome gives the session a finish line.

## Use both without adding another dashboard

The practical combination is simple:

1. Write one observable outcome.
2. Choose a duration that matches the energy and complexity of the task.
3. Start the timer and keep the outcome visible.
4. Record what moved forward, not only how many minutes passed.

## Which should you choose?

Choose a timer when starting is the main problem. Choose a focus block when the task is complex and you need a concrete definition of done. For most deep work, use the block as the unit and the Pomodoro as the attention boundary.
